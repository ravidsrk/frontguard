## Does “Beyond Pixel Diffs: Benchmarking Image Change Captioning for Web UI Visual Regression Testing” exist, and what does WUICC-bench show about pixel diffs versus trained captioners and zero-shot LLMs?

### Takeaway
The paper is real (arXiv:2607.01728, 2 Jul 2026). On WUICC-bench, naive pixel comparison flags almost every non-meaningful change, trained image-difference captioners suppress that noise while still reporting most single meaningful edits, and two zero-shot VLMs do not hold both sides of that tradeoff. Caption quality stays low, and the data are single synthetic edits on LLM-made pages, so the result is not a pass/fail replacement for pixelmatch.

### Cited Findings
- Title, authors, venue, date: “Beyond Pixel Diffs: Benchmarking Image Change Captioning for Web UI Visual Regression Testing,” Licheng Zhang, Bach Le, Pengtao Zhao, Naveed Akhtar (University of Melbourne). arXiv:2607.01728v1 [cs.CV], submitted 2 Jul 2026. No conference acceptance is stated on the HTML page. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01728 and HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01728v1 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01728v1)
- Task and data: Web UI Image Change Captioning (WUICC) and WUICC-bench. Each sample is one atomic change from a 37-rule taxonomy (nine meaningful categories, three non-meaningful). Pages come from WebSight, a corpus of LLM-synthesized HTML, mutated by GPT-5.1, rendered in a headless browser at viewport width 1280, and kept only if a human finds the caption completely and accurately describes the rendered change. 9,906 human-verified samples: 8,583 meaningful and 1,323 non-meaningful. Split approximately 70:10:20, giving 6,963 train, 982 validation, and 1,961 test samples. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01728v1)
- Reliability of the labels, not of a regression detector: on 100 samples deliberately oversampled for invalid generations, two annotators agreed on 86 (47 valid by both, 39 invalid by both); Cohen’s κ ≈ 0.722. Over the full generation pool, 78.09% of samples were retained as correct. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01728v1)
- Eleven IDC methods were reimplemented and trained on WUICC-bench (Chg2Cap, SEN, ICT-Net, RSICCformer, RSCaMa, SparseFocus, DAE, RMNet, CARD, VARD, SCORER). Two zero-shot VLMs, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct and Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, were not trained on the bench. Captions capped at 50 words; beam width 3; scores on the test split. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01728v1)
- Table V caption metrics (higher is better). Best n-gram/CIDEr is SparseFocus: BLEU-1 0.4725, BLEU-2 0.3779, BLEU-3 0.3093, BLEU-4 0.2571, METEOR 0.2438, ROUGE_L 0.5060, CIDEr 2.0761, SPICE 0.2579. Best SPICE is Chg2Cap at 0.3345 (BLEU-4 0.2429, CIDEr 1.9492, ROUGE_L 0.4876). Lowest trained BLEU-4 is SCORER at 0.1373. Zero-shot Llama-3.2-11B: BLEU-4 0.0363, ROUGE_L 0.2624, CIDEr 0.4860, SPICE 0.0861. Zero-shot Qwen2-VL-7B: BLEU-4 0.0920, ROUGE_L 0.3949, CIDEr 1.8699, SPICE 0.2498. The paper says absolute scores remain low for both trained methods and off-the-shelf VLMs. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01728v1)
- Table VI change-awareness accuracy (%), only four trained methods plus the two VLMs and two pixel references, not all eleven. “No-Change Acc.” is the share of non-meaningful samples described as “no change.” “Change Acc.” is the share of meaningful samples reported as changed. Pixel Diff: no-change 0.00, change 100.00. Pixel Diff with a 0.1 difference threshold and anti-aliasing tolerance: no-change 6.72, change 97.93. SEN: 96.27 / 96.16. ICT-Net: 97.01 / 93.80. RSICCformer: 85.82 / 95.33. RMNet: 97.39 / 93.55. Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct: 21.64 / 79.39. Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct: 99.63 / 65.68. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01728v1)
- The paper’s own reading of Table VI: pixel comparison flags almost every meaningful change and suppresses almost nothing; a fixed tolerance lifts suppression by under seven points and starts missing genuine changes. Trained IDC no-change accuracy is 85.82–97.39% with change accuracy still above 93%. Qwen2-VL’s 99.63% suppression comes with only 65.68% change accuracy. Llama fails the other way (21.64% no-change). The authors say off-the-shelf VLMs do not yet give the selective suppression VRT needs. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01728v1)
- Limitations stated in the paper: captions are LLM-written, then human-checked, so lexical metrics can favor the generator’s phrasing (mitigated, they argue, because all eleven methods share the references and the two VLMs are not GPT-family). BLEU/CIDEr/ROUGE reward surface overlap; a one-token text error is diluted because the changed text is a small part of the caption. External validity: WebSight is not framework-heavy production UI and is not JavaScript-driven dynamic content. Each sample is exactly one taxonomy change; compound regressions are out of scope. Change-Agent, ChangeChat, and TEOChat were not run because they need masks or boxes the bench does not provide. GPT-5 and Claude were not evaluated. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01728v1)

### Inferences
- WUICC-bench is the closest public web-UI comparison of pixel diffs, trained change captioners, and zero-shot VLMs. It supports using a captioner as an advisory description after pixelmatch fires, not as the gate. The pixel-diff arm is a reference inside a synthetic captioning task. A 0% no-change score is what you get when “non-meaningful” samples are defined as visible pixel edits (small shifts, padding, subtle style, transient text). It is not a measured false-positive rate of pixelmatch on real repositories.
- Even the best trained suppressors are not a drop-in oracle: best BLEU-4 is 0.2571, only four architectures appear in the detection table, and the authors leave production pages and multi-edit releases to future work. A model that says “no change” on 3–14% of meaningful single edits (change accuracy 93.55–96.16) would hide real regressions if it replaced pixelmatch.
- Zero-shot Llama and Qwen2-VL are the wrong shape for a gate. One under-suppresses noise; the other buys suppression by missing about a third of meaningful changes. That matches Frontguard’s rule that model output stays advisory.

### Gaps
- No production CI corpus, no multi-edit releases, and no closed frontier VLMs are in this paper. The size of Design-like real-site transfer is explicitly left open by the authors.
- Industry tools named in the introduction (Applitools Eyes, Percy, Chromatic) are described as proprietary and without public evaluation. This note does not add numbers for them.
- Older VRT papers that WUICC cites (Adachi et al., APSEC 2018, redundant-check reduction; Tanno et al., Journal of Information Processing 2020, region-based essential differences; Adachi et al., COMPSAC 2020, masking dynamic areas; Aridome et al., MIXVRT prototype 2025 and MIXVRT journal 2026) were not opened. Their metrics are unverified here. Bibliographic facts are only as printed in the WUICC reference list. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01728v1)

## Does “LLMShot: Reducing snapshot testing maintenance via LLMs” exist, what model sizes and dataset size did they use, and what were the exact hit/recall/precision/F1 figures? What failed?

### Takeaway
LLMShot is real (arXiv:2507.10062v2, 24 Jul 2025). On 17 snapshot failures from one purpose-built iOS app, Gemma 3 12B reaches hit 82.35%, recall 84.21%, precision 66.67%, F1 74.42%; Gemma 3 4B is lower. A prompt that tells the model to ignore a category collapses. This is an iOS failure-triage study, not a web pixel-diff gate.

### Cited Findings
- Title and authors: “LLMShot: Reducing snapshot testing maintenance via LLMs,” Ergün Batuhan Kaynak, Mayasah Lami, Sahand Moslemi, Anil Koyuncu (Bilkent University). arXiv:2507.10062v2 [cs.SE], 24 Jul 2025 (v1 was 14 Jul 2025). The HTML does not name a conference. A Zenodo software record calls it an accepted paper and links the arXiv id, but the venue name was not on that record page. Code link printed there: https://github.com/SahandMoslemi/LLMShot. Paper’s dataset footnote: https://zenodo.org/records/15876539 (that record was not re-opened). Zenodo record actually opened: https://zenodo.org/records/16889941. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10062v2); [Zenodo v4](https://zenodo.org/records/16889941)
- What it does: a VLM reads reference, failure, and pixel-diff images and emits JSON (categories, a 0–1 pixel-diff estimate, a 0–1 semantic-diff score, affected elements, a short explanation). It is applied to snapshot failures, not to a mixed pass/fail stream. The authors say it is unlike existing pass/fail approaches. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10062v2)
- Dataset size: one purpose-built SwiftUI iOS app, 19 runtime feature flags, 17 XCTest/XCUITest cases (two of them multi-flag), captured with swift-snapshot-testing on Xcode 16.3, iPhone 15 Pro, iOS 17.2. Each test is run once for a baseline and once with a flag to force a failure. Two authors labeled categories and resolved disagreements. Category counts across the 17 tests: COLOR_CHANGE in 5, PADDING_CHANGE, CONTENT_CHANGE, and LAYOUT_CHANGE in 3 each, TEXT_CHANGE and ANIMATION_PHASE in 2 each, ANIMATION_CHANGE in 1. Mean normalized pixel difference 0.053 ± 0.054. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10062v2)
- Models: Gemma 3 4B and 12B, temperature 0.1, via Ollama, because they accept multi-image prompts. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10062v2)
- Table II, classification of failure root causes. Hit rate is the share of test cases with at least one correct ground-truth category. gemma3:4b: hit 76.47%, recall 78.95%, precision 57.69%, F1 66.67%, average labels per test 1.53 ± 0.50, unknown rate 11.76%. gemma3:12b: hit 82.35%, recall 84.21%, precision 66.67%, F1 74.42%, average labels 1.41 ± 0.49, unknown rate 5.88%. Pixel-difference absolute error: 0.095 ± 0.161 (4B) and 0.059 ± 0.048 (12B). Semantic difference score: 0.921 ± 0.046 (4B) versus 0.079 ± 0.079 (12B). — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10062v2)
- What failed: the prompt-based ignore extension, evaluated only with gemma3:4b. “Ignore From Analysis” (IFA) tells the model to ignore the category it first predicted. “Ignore From Ground Truth” (IFGT) tells it to ignore a known true category. Table III: IFA hit 18.75%, recall 28.57%, precision 9.09%, F1 13.79%, unknown rate 25.00%, ignore-compliance 31.25%. IFGT hit 41.18%, recall 100.00%, precision 9.09%, F1 16.67%, unknown rate 11.76%, ignore-compliance 35.29%. Pixel-error rose to 0.157 ± 0.244 (IFA) and 0.287 ± 0.339 (IFGT) from the 4B baseline 0.095 ± 0.161. The authors call the 100% IFGT recall misleading because precision is 9.09%, and they say prompting is insufficient for controllable visual reasoning. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10062v2)
- Threats the authors state: one iOS app, so results may not transfer to Android, web, or other design systems; the taxonomy may be incomplete; labels are from two authors; no head-to-head comparison with DiffDroid; LLM randomness is only partly controlled by temperature 0.1. They also say the community needs larger datasets mined from real project histories, and user studies of whether the explanations help. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10062v2)

### Inferences
- Do not generalize this iOS study to web pixelmatch. Seventeen failures, one app, author-made flags, and precision stuck at 66.67% even for the larger model. A wrong category on about one in three predicted labels is advisory noise, not a ship decision.
- The semantic-diff score is not a threshold. The 4B and 12B models report 0.921 versus 0.079 on the same task. Any Frontguard judge that exposed a similar scalar as pass/fail would be using an uncalibrated number.
- Prompt-based “ignore this kind of change” is the experiment that most closely matches a reviewer wanting the model to waive an expected diff. It failed on the only model they tried (4B): compliance about 31–35%, and F1 fell to 13.79% or 16.67%. That is evidence against letting a prompt suppress pixelmatch failures.

### Gaps
- The acceptance venue is unverified. The arXiv HTML does not name one. Zenodo says “Accepted” without a venue in the text that was opened.
- The in-paper Zenodo id 15876539 was not opened, so the file inventory of that record is unverified. The opened record is 16889941.
- Papers LLMShot cites but that were not opened: Fujita et al., ICSME 2023, empirical study of snapshot testing; Cruz, Rocha, and Valente, Journal of Systems and Software 2023, “Snapshot testing in practice”; Bui and Rocha, MSR 2023, a snapshot-testing dataset the authors describe as static project attributes without executed tests or images. Metrics from those three are not reported here.

## What does VisRefiner claim, and why is it an evaluation target for an experimental judge rather than a gate?

### Takeaway
VisRefiner (arXiv:2602.05998, 5 Feb 2026) is a screenshot-to-code trainer. It learns from visual differences between a rendered prediction and a reference design, then self-refines the generated HTML. Its scores are layout-fidelity scores for generated UI code, not precision or recall of regressions. That makes the benchmark a place to probe an experimental judge, not a release gate.

### Cited Findings
- Title and authors: “VisRefiner: Learning from Visual Differences for Screenshot-to-Code Generation,” Jie Deng, Kaichun Yao, Libo Zhang (Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences; Deng also University of Chinese Academy of Sciences). arXiv:2602.05998v1 [cs.CV], 5 Feb 2026, CC BY 4.0. No conference is named on the HTML. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05998 and https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05998v1 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05998v1)
- Claim, from the abstract and method: existing MLLMs map a screenshot to code without seeing the render of that code. VisRefiner builds difference-aligned supervision that ties visual discrepancies to code edits, then uses GRPO so the model can look at its own render and the target and edit the code. Perturbations cover color, layout, alignment, component, image, and text. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05998v1)
- Data and training actually opened: SFT on about 20k difference-aligned pairs for 1 epoch; GRPO on 400 structurally challenging unpaired examples for 8 epochs; both on 8×80G A100 (about 6h and 20h). Table of partitions: SFT 20K paired, mean code tokens 1.95K, DOM depth 8.3, blocks 16.4; RL 400 unpaired, tokens 2.77K, DOM 10.2, blocks 28.1; eval 100 unpaired, tokens 2.08K, DOM 8.4, blocks 16.0. HTML is rendered with headless Chromium. Design2Code has 484 real-world screenshots with reference HTML. Design2Code-HARD is described as denser layouts; its sample count was not in the passage opened. VisDiffUI-Test has 100 examples. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05998v1)
- Table 2 single-step averages (Block, Text, Position, Color, CLIP, then AVG), one decimal. Design2Code: VisRefiner-7B 90.6 / 97.5 / 81.7 / 83.1 / 90.1 / AVG 88.6, against GPT-4o AVG 90.2, Qwen2.5-VL-72B 88.2, Qwen2.5-VL-7B 85.4. After self-refinement, VisRefiner-7B AVG 89.3 (+0.7) and GPT-4o AVG 89.9 (−0.3). Design2Code-HARD: VisRefiner-7B 78.1 / 96.2 / 82.3 / 83.7 / 87.9 / AVG 85.6, against GPT-4o 78.8, Qwen2.5-VL-72B 80.8, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 81.9. Self-refinement: VisRefiner-7B 86.3 (+0.7); GPT-4o 84.0 (+5.2). The VisDiffUI-TEST numeric block was cut off before it could be copied, so those cells are not reported here. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05998v1)
- The reward is not a defect decision. Format reward penalizes invalid HTML/CSS. Refinement reward fires if the new render improves visual similarity. The paper does not report hit, recall, precision, or F1 for regression detection. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05998v1)

### Inferences
- VisRefiner is an evaluation target for an experimental judge because it publishes paired renders, controlled visual edits, and numeric layout scores a judge could be asked to explain. It is not a gate because success is “generated code looks like the design,” measured by block/text/position/color/CLIP, on screenshot-to-code benchmarks. A high score does not say whether a product screenshot differs from an approved baseline for an acceptable reason.
- Self-refinement is not monotone. On Design2Code, GPT-4o’s AVG fell by 0.3 after refinement while VisRefiner-7B rose by 0.7. A loop that edits code from a visual diff can move the wrong way. That is another reason not to let this model family accept or reject a Frontguard run.
- The 7B model beating GPT-4o on Design2Code-HARD block match (78.1 vs 56.6) is a code-generation result. It must not be quoted as regression-detection accuracy.

### Gaps
- Design2Code-HARD’s example count and the VisDiffUI-TEST table numbers were not captured from the HTML before truncation. They are unverified here.
- No human-evaluation percentages were extracted. The HTML table of contents lists a human study of refinement behavior; the figures were not opened.
- No statement was found that VisRefiner should be used in CI. Any “judge versus gate” conclusion is an inference from the task definition, not a claim the authors make about visual regression testing.

## Which other real papers are relevant, and which are only adjacent?

### Takeaway
DiffDroid, Owl Eyes, Nighthawk, GLIB, dVermin (Metamorphosis), and Trident (Seeing is Believing) are real mobile or game UI-defect papers. They are adjacent to web baseline VRT: they detect display bugs, scaling bugs, game glitches, cross-device inconsistency, or multi-step functional bugs, and they do not replace a pixelmatch gate on web screenshots. XBIDetective is the closest extra web paper: cross-browser screenshot pairs judged by a VLM, with accuracy well below a safe gate. Perceptual-hash writeups found here are not UI-regression oracles.

### Cited Findings

#### Relevant or nearest neighbors (still not a pixelmatch replacement)

- XBIDetective. “XBIDetective: Leveraging Vision Language Models for Identifying Cross-Browser Visual Inconsistencies,” Balreet Grewal, James Graham, Jeff Muizelaar, Jan Honza Odvarko, Suhaib Mujahid, Marco Castelluccio, Cor-Paul Bezemer. arXiv:2512.15804v1, 16 Dec 2025. ICSE-SEIP 2026 (12–18 Apr 2026, Rio de Janeiro), confirmed on the arXiv HTML header and the ICSE 2026 program page. The tool screenshots a site in Firefox and Chrome and asks a VLM about cross-browser inconsistencies. On 1,052 websites, the fine-tuned VLM identifies discrepancies with 79% accuracy and detects dynamic elements and advertisements with 84% and 85% accuracy. The HTML conclusion also says the base model reaches 77% accuracy and the fine-tuned model 79%. A separate run on 1,695 websites had no ground truth; the authors say false positives from dynamic elements and pop-ups must be reduced, and that comparable screenshots across browsers are hard to capture. Why it is not a gate: the pair is two browsers, not baseline versus candidate build; 79% accuracy leaves a large error rate; dynamic content is an explicit failure mode. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15804 — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15804); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15804v1); [ICSE 2026 SEIP](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2026/icse-2026-software-engineering-in-practice/38/XBIDetective-Leveraging-Vision-Language-Models-for-Identifying-Cross-Browser-Visual-)

### Inferences
- The mobile display-bug line (Owl Eyes, Nighthawk, dVermin, GLIB) reports high detection F1 because the positive class is a visible glitch in one image or in a scale pair. Frontguard’s hard case is different: both images can be well-formed, and the question is whether the difference is an intended update. LLMShot and WUICC are the papers that actually pose that question. Neither authorizes replacing pixelmatch.
- Cross-paper F1 must not be pooled. Owl Eyes’ abstract 85/84 and Nighthawk’s re-run of OwlEye at 0.790/0.778 are different protocols. GLIB’s 100% precision is on games with existing bug reports. dVermin’s 97% is scale inconsistency. XBIDetective’s 79% is cross-browser accuracy. None of these is a web pixelmatch false-positive rate.
- For an experimental Frontguard judge, the usable public targets are WUICC-bench (web change captions and no-change suppression), LLMShot’s 17-case taxonomy (explanation quality, with the ignore failure as a negative control), and only as a side probe VisRefiner’s render-versus-design pairs. XBIDetective is a cautionary web result: a fine-tuned VLM on real sites still sits at 79% accuracy and still trips on dynamic content.

### Gaps
- DiffDroid’s precision and false-positive count were not in the PDF pages opened. “Limited number of false positives” and “96 inconsistencies” are the only quantitative claims copied.
- Nighthawk’s 0.84/0.84 and 64,000-screenshot figures come from the ar5iv HTML. The abstract-page fetch confirmed the title and authors, then truncated before the metric sentence; the metrics are cited from the ar5iv rendering of the same id, not retyped from a PDF.
- MIXVRT (2025 prototype, 2026 journal article) is the most on-scope web VRT system named inside WUICC, and it was not opened. Its layout-defect numbers are unverified.
- No paper opened here reports a calibrated probability that a human would accept a pixelmatch diff. That quantity is the one a gate would need, and it is absent.
